# Your first book

## The start screen

Draftline opens on the start screen. It lists the books you have been working
on, most recent first, each showing its cover art if one of its editions has
any and its own colour if not.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: getting-started/start-screen.png
     The start screen with two or more recent books — at least one showing
     cover art and one showing its colour — plus the New Book and Open Book
     buttons. Invented titles only. -->

Click a book to open it. Right-click one for the rest, including removing it
from the list — which does not delete anything.

## Making a book

**New Book** asks two things: a **title** and an **author**. Neither is
permanent; both live in **Book Details & Editions** afterwards and can be
changed whenever you like.

You then choose where to save it. That is the moment the book becomes a file
on your disk, and after that Draftline saves into it by itself.

## Importing a book you already have

The same dialog offers **Import DOCX** and **Import EPUB**. Draftline reads
the file, shows you what it found — how many chapters, and how it split them
— and only then creates the book.

Look at that preview. Chapter detection is guesswork on somebody else's file,
and it is far easier to correct before you start writing than after. If it has
divided the book badly, cancel, and try again after fixing the headings in the
original.

## What the file is

One book is one file, ending in `.draftline`. Inside it are your chapters,
your front and back matter, your planner, your cast, your editions and their
cover art, and the earlier versions of chapters you have revised.

That matters for three reasons:

- **Backing up a book is copying one file.** No folder to keep together, no
  library to export.
- **Moving a book is moving one file**, to another machine or another disk.
- **Nothing lives in the cloud** unless you put it there yourself. Draftline
  has no account and no server.

You can open a `.draftline` by double-clicking it, or by dragging it onto the
Draftline window.

## Saving

Draftline saves a few seconds after you stop typing. The status bar along the
bottom says when it last did.

There is no Save button, and no way to be in a document you have not saved.
**Save & Quit** in the menu saves and exits; **Back to Library** saves and
returns to the start screen. Both refuse to leave if the save fails, so you
cannot walk away from work that did not make it to disk.

## One book, one window

The same book cannot be open in two windows at once — the second is refused
and the window already holding it comes forward instead. Two *different* books
side by side is fine, and **New Window** in the menu is how you get there.
