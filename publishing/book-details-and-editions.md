# Book details and editions

**Book Details & Editions** in the menu. Two things live here: what the book
is, and every version of it you have published.

## The book

Title, author or pen name, and the details a shop asks for — blurb, series,
categories. The storefront details are folded away until you want them,
because most days you do not.

There are no ISBNs here. An ISBN belongs to an edition and a format, not to a
book, which is why they live one level down.

## Editions

An edition is one publication of the book: the first edition, a revised second
edition, a large-print reissue. It carries a copyright year, a status, and the
formats it is published in.

Each **format** within it — paperback, hardcover, ebook, audiobook — has its
own ISBN, because that is how ISBNs work. One book with a paperback, a
hardcover and an ebook is three ISBNs, and a second edition is three more.

An edition can record which edition it supersedes, and the copyright page
then prints the earlier years along with its own, the way a reissue should.

Nothing is required. You can export a book that has no editions at all.

## Cover art

Attach cover art to an edition and it appears on that edition, in the export,
and on the start screen for the whole book.

Artwork is chosen by picking the file. Draftline reads what it is — size,
resolution, colour — and reports it, so a cover that is too small for print
is something you find out now.

## Print-ready wrap artwork

A printed book needs a wrap: front, spine and back as one piece, at the
printer's measurements. Attach it per format, because a paperback wrap and a
hardcover wrap are different files.

A wrap is tens of megabytes, so Draftline asks whether to keep a copy inside
the book or link to it where it lives. It tells you the real numbers when it
asks.

**Keeping a copy** means the book carries its artwork anywhere, and the file
grows accordingly — including for every sync and every backup.
**Linking** keeps the book small, and needs the original still to be there
when you export.

Neither is wrong. If the book lives in a sync folder, linking is usually
kinder.
