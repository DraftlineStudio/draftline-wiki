# Snapshots

A snapshot is the manuscript, frozen, kept inside the book.

## Why

You publish. You carry on writing — a typo here, a line of dialogue there.
Six months later someone asks what is on page 214 of the paperback, or your
printer wants the file again, and the manuscript in front of you is no longer
the one that went out.

A snapshot is the answer to "what did the first edition actually say". It is
tied to the edition it belongs to, so the ISBN, the text and the files that
were made from it stay together.

## Taking one

From **[Book details and
editions](/publishing/book-details-and-editions)**, on the edition concerned.
Draftline copies the manuscript as it stands into the book and records when.

You can take as many as you like. They are text, so they cost almost nothing.

## Releasing one, and the three confirmations

Releasing a snapshot marks it as the text an edition was published with, and
**it cannot be undone**. Draftline asks three times.

That is deliberate, and it is not there to be annoying. A released snapshot is
a statement about a book that exists in the world with an ISBN on it. Everyone
who builds one of these eventually meets a writer who clicked through a dialog
at one in the morning and destroyed the only record of what their first
edition said.

Three confirmations is the cost of never being that writer. Read them; they
say different things.

## Afterwards

The released snapshot stays with its edition. Export from it and you get the
published text, not today's draft, however much you have written since.

Your working manuscript is untouched throughout. A snapshot is a copy, and
taking or releasing one never changes what you are writing.
