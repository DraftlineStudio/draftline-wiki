# Exporting

**Export** in the menu. There are two ways through it, and which one you want
depends on whether you are sending somebody a file or publishing a book.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: publishing/export-start.png
     The first step of the export flow, showing the choice between exporting an
     edition and choosing formats individually. -->

## Sending somebody a file

Choose the formats you want, set them up, review, and export. You get the
files themselves, where you asked for them.

This is the one you want for an editor, a beta reader, or a copy for your
phone.

## Publishing an edition

Choose an edition instead, and Draftline exports everything that edition is
made of as **one publication-ready zip**: a folder per format, each holding
the interior file and that format's artwork.

That shape is not decoration. It is what you hand to a printer or upload to a
shop, and it means the paperback interior and the paperback cover cannot get
separated on the way.

Editions come from **[Book details and
editions](/publishing/book-details-and-editions)**.

## The formats

| | |
|---|---|
| **EPUB** | For shops and e-readers. EPUB 3 by default; EPUB 2 for older devices that refuse anything else. |
| **DOCX** | For an editor. Track changes can be on, so what comes back is markable. |
| **Reading copy PDF** | To read or to send. Not for a printer. |
| **Print-ready PDF** | For a printer: real trim size, real margins, crop marks. |
| **Hardcover interior** | The same, set for a hardcover's different measurements. |
| **Audiobook script** | A narration copy, not a reading copy. Spaced and marked for someone reading aloud into a microphone. |

## Typesetting

Print formats ask how the book should be set: trim size, font, alignment,
chapter headings, scene breaks, running heads and page numbers.

**Industry standard** applies the settings a professionally typeset novel
uses, and is the right answer unless you know why it is not. **Custom** opens
all of it.

Changing a setting that industry standard has an opinion about switches you to
custom, and says so. Nothing is decided behind your back.

## Before the final one

The last step lists everything that is about to be written, and where. Read
it. A print-ready PDF at the wrong trim size is a mistake you discover after
paying for proofs.

For anything going to a printer or a shop, freeze the text first — see
**[Snapshots](/publishing/snapshots)**. That way the file you uploaded and the
text you uploaded stay tied together after you carry on writing.
