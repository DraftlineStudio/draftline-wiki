# Settings

**Settings** in the menu. Six sections.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: reference/settings.png
     The settings dialog open on Application, showing the section list down
     the side. -->

## Application

**Theme** — light, dark, or automatic. Automatic follows the clock, and you
can set your own dawn and dusk rather than take the defaults.

**Default save location** — where the file dialog starts when you make a book.

**Check for updates automatically** — on by default. It asks the update
service for the newest version and tells you if there is one. Nothing
downloads until you say so, and nothing about you or your books is sent. Turn
it off and you can still check by hand.

**Background analysis** — whether Draftline reads the manuscript while you
write to keep the cast and the readings current. Turning it off makes those
views stop updating on their own; it does not remove them.

## Author

Your default author name, publisher and copyright line. These prefill a new
book so you are not typing your own name every time. Each book keeps its own
copy afterwards, so changing this does not rewrite books you have already
made — a pen name stays with the book that used it.

## Book

Two different things, which is worth knowing before you change one.

**Editor display** — text size and line spacing *while you write*. Affects
nothing you export. Make the text comfortable; it costs you nothing.

**Export settings** — the defaults a new export starts from: trim size, drop
caps, and the rest. These are starting points, and the export flow can
override every one for a particular export.

## AI Studio

Off unless you turn it on, and it needs a key or a tool of your own. Draftline
ships no AI and includes no service.

Supported: Claude, Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, and a local model on your own
machine. A local model sends nothing anywhere.

Nothing is sent to any provider unless you ask for something specific, on the
text you asked about. Draftline does not send your manuscript anywhere in the
background, with AI on or off.

## Read Aloud

Off until enabled, because enabling it downloads a voice model — once, then it
is offline for good. See **[Read Aloud](/writing/read-aloud)**.
