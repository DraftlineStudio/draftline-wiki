# The editor

One chapter at a time, on a page, at a width chosen for reading.

## Formatting

The toolbar has what a novel needs: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough,
superscript and subscript, alignment, lists, block quotes, and a paragraph
style selector for headings.

**Ctrl+B**, **Ctrl+I** and **Ctrl+U** do the obvious three. The rest are on
the toolbar — see **[Keyboard shortcuts](/reference/keyboard-shortcuts)**.

Emphasis is the only formatting most novels use. If you find yourself reaching
for much else, it is worth asking whether it belongs in the manuscript or in
the design of the edition.

## Scene breaks

A scene break is a real thing in Draftline, not three asterisks you typed.
Insert one from the toolbar and it stays a scene break through every export,
where each format renders it as it should: an asterism in one book, a blank
line in another, a small rule in a third. You choose that when you export.

Typing `***` yourself gives you three asterisks, and they will be three
asterisks in the printed book too.

## What is deliberately absent

No ruler, no margins, no page size, no line spacing, no style gallery.

Not an omission. How the book *looks* is a decision about the edition, made
once, in the export flow — where you can see it applied to the whole book at
the size it will be printed. Setting it per paragraph while writing gives you
a manuscript that looks like a finished book and sets like neither.

The editor font and size in **[Settings](/reference/settings)** change what
*you* look at while writing. They have no effect on anything exported.

## Undo

A hundred steps deep, per chapter, reset when you move to another chapter. For
anything older than that, see
**[Chapter history](/writing/chapter-history)**.

## Saving

A few seconds after you stop typing. The status bar says when it last did, and
there is no Save button because there is nothing to save by hand.
