# Read Aloud

Draftline can read your manuscript aloud, on your own machine, with no
internet connection once it is set up.

## Why bother

Prose that reads fine on the page falls over when spoken. Repeated words you
stopped seeing three drafts ago, a sentence that runs out of air, dialogue
nobody would say — hearing a chapter is the cheapest edit there is.

## Turning it on

**Settings → Read Aloud**. Enabling it downloads a voice model once. After
that it runs offline: nothing is sent anywhere, and your manuscript does not
leave the machine.

The download is not small. It is also once.

## Listening

Start it and follow along in the editor. Speed is adjustable — faster than
natural for a scan, slower for a close pass.

## Voices for characters

Dialogue can be read in different voices, assigned per character. This is
worth the setup for one reason: a scene where two people sound identical is a
scene where readers lose track of who is speaking, and hearing it in two
voices makes that obvious immediately.

Characters come from your cast — see
**[Characters](/planning/characters)**.

## What to expect

These are synthetic voices running on your computer. They are clear and
steady, and they are not narration. Unusual names will be mispronounced, and
invented ones almost certainly.

Use it to hear the rhythm of your sentences, not to audition a narrator.
