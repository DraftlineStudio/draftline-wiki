# Chapters and the manuscript

The left panel is your book in reading order, in three groups.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: writing/chapter-panel.png
     The left panel with the Manuscript tab active, showing front matter, a
     body of several chapters, and back matter. Invented titles. -->

**Front matter** is everything before the story: a title page, a dedication,
an epigraph. **The body** is the book. **Back matter** is what follows: an
afterword, acknowledgements, an about-the-author.

The division is not decoration. It decides what gets a chapter number, what
appears in a table of contents, and how each part is set when you export an
edition. A dedication numbered "Chapter 1" is the thing this prevents.

## Adding and organising

Add a chapter with the button at the head of its group. Drag to reorder;
numbering follows, so moving chapter nine above chapter three renumbers both
and everything between.

Right-click a chapter for the rest — rename, duplicate, delete, and move it to
another group. Moving a chapter into front matter takes its number away,
because front matter is not numbered.

## Naming

A chapter has a title, and it is allowed to be nothing. Plenty of novels have
chapters called only "1", and plenty have none at all.

What you type is what gets set, so `Chapter One` prints as "Chapter One" and
`One` prints as "One". Draftline does not add the word "Chapter" for you; how
chapter headings are formatted is decided once when you export, not here.

## Word counts

The status bar counts the chapter you are in. The **Dashboard** in the tool
rail counts the book, the groups and each chapter, so you can see the shape of
it — which chapters ran long, which are thin, and whether the middle sags.

Front and back matter are counted separately from the body, because a
seventy-thousand-word novel is seventy thousand words of story, not of story
plus acknowledgements.

## Deleting

Deleting a chapter asks first, and takes its history with it. If you only want
it out of the way, move it to back matter and leave it there — nothing forces
you to export what is in the book.
