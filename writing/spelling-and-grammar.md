# Spelling and grammar

Both are on by default and both can be turned off in
**[Settings](/reference/settings)**.

## Spelling

Misspelled words are underlined as you type. Right-click for suggestions, or
to add the word to your dictionary.

Draftline checks against American English unless you tell it otherwise.
**English variant** in **[Settings](/reference/settings)**, under
Application, switches to British, Canadian, or Australian English: colour
and honour stop being underlined, color and honor start, and the -ise and
-ize endings follow the convention you chose. If you use AI editing, it
follows the same choice, so a copy edit corrects toward your spelling
rather than away from it.

**Add to dictionary** is the one that matters for fiction. A novel is full of
words no dictionary has: invented names, invented places, invented things.
Add them once and they stop being underlined everywhere.

Your added words are yours, not the book's, so they follow you across every
book you write. A series keeps its vocabulary.

A confirmed character's name typed with a bare s on the end — Mara's
written as Maras — is underlined too, and the possessive is the first
suggestion. The dictionary alone cannot catch that one, because many names
plus an s are themselves legitimate surnames.

## Grammar

Likely mistakes get a blue underline: a doubled word, a sentence or the
pronoun I left lowercase, a contraction missing its apostrophe (im, dont),
its where it's was meant, Were opening a sentence in place of We're, a
stray capital after a comma, the wrong article before a word, a period
trailing an exclamation or question mark.

Dialogue gets its own check: a quote that ends in a period when a dialogue
tag follows — "Leave it." she said — is marked, and the comma is offered.

Some typography corrects itself as you type: straight quotes curl into
literary quotes, three periods become an ellipsis (…), and two hyphens
become an em dash (—). No special keys to find.

Pasted or imported text keeps whatever it arrived with, so straight quotes
in it are underlined individually — or right-click anywhere in the chapter
and choose **Fix typography in this chapter** to convert all of them at
once. One undo brings the chapter back exactly as it was.

## The Proofread panel

The **Proofread** glyph in the sidebar collects everything in one list:
every spelling, grammar, and style detection in the open chapter, filterable
by kind, with counts. Click one and it expands in place — the passage in
context, the fixes as buttons, and the chapter scrolls to spotlight the
exact spot. Apply a fix, ignore it, add a word to your dictionary, or
silence a style suggestion for good; the arrow keys move between
detections, Enter applies the first fix, Escape ignores. When straight
quotes or typed dashes are present, the footer offers to fix all typography
in one click.

The gear in the panel opens **Checks & Dictionary**: switches for the three
checks, your custom dictionary, and a list of everything you have silenced,
each with a way back.

Style suggestions use a dotted amber underline: three typed periods where
an ellipsis character belongs (in pasted or imported text the conversion
above cannot reach), stacked exclamation marks, an "its" before a verb that
only probably means "it's." They are suggestions, nothing more.

When story analysis is on, narration also gets gentle regional-vocabulary
hints: a word like bubbler or davenport is underlined with a note about
where it is understood and what a broader audience would call it. Keep
original is always valid, and dialogue is never touched — a character who
says bubbler is from somewhere, and that is the point.

Story analysis also reads for echoes: a noticeable word
repeated within a few lines of narration gets the same dotted underline,
with the distance in the tooltip. Dialogue is exempt — repetition in speech
is a voice, not a slip — and so are your characters' names and any word the
chapter uses throughout, the way a story told through a camera keeps saying
camera. Right-click an echo to dismiss that one suggestion, or to never
flag that word again anywhere in the book. A dismissed suggestion stays
gone until the passage itself changes.

Fiction breaks grammatical rules constantly and on purpose. Sentence
fragments. Dialogue that does not parse. A paragraph that is one word long.

So treat grammar marks as a second pair of eyes with no taste — useful for
the doubled "the the" you cannot see any more, not for whether a sentence
should stand. Ignore it freely. It keeps no score.

## What neither will do

Neither rewrites anything. Nothing changes in your manuscript unless you
change it.

If you want prose *suggestions* rather than spelling, that is AI Studio, it is
off by default, and it needs a key of your own — see
**[Settings](/reference/settings)**.
