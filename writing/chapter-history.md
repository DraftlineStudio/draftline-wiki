# Chapter history

Draftline keeps earlier versions of chapters as you revise them, inside the
book. **Chapter History** in the menu shows them for the chapter you are in.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: writing/chapter-history.png
     The chapter history dialog listing several saved versions with dates, one
     selected and showing its text. -->

## What it is for

Undo covers the last hundred steps in the chapter you are working on, and
forgets when you move away. History covers the rest: the draft from before
last week's rewrite, the version where that scene still had the argument in
it, the paragraph you cut on Tuesday and now want back.

## Using it

Pick a version to see it. You can copy what you need out of it, or restore the
whole chapter to that version.

Restoring does not lose anything — the current text becomes a version in the
history too, so you can restore back to where you were if the older draft
turns out to be worse than you remembered.

## What it is not

It is not a backup. History lives inside the `.draftline` file, so it protects
you from your own edits, not from losing the file. For that, copy the file —
see **[Your files](/reference/your-files)**.

It is also not a full record of every keystroke. Versions are saved as you
work rather than continuously, so history has the shape of your drafting
sessions rather than of every save.
